## What I'd Avoid and Why

- **Long-Duration US Treasury Bonds:** With core PCE at 3.1% and re-accelerating, holding 10-year nominal Treasuries yielding 4.44% provides barely positive real returns in the best case and active capital destruction if inflation stays elevated. For a perpetual institution, locking in 1.3% real yield when real assets and private credit offer 8–12% nominal returns is an opportunity cost that compounds against us every year. Bonds are an anchor. I have said this for 40 years and the mathematics have not changed.

- **US Large-Cap Active Equity Managers:** CAPE at 36.7x means the market is pricing perfection in US equities. Paying an active manager 75–100 basis points per year to underperform the index in a structurally efficient market — which is what the data says they do on average, persistently — is doubly inexcusable when the starting valuation provides so little upside cushion. If we hold US equity, we hold SPY or VOO at 3 basis points. We do not pay for beta.

- **High-Yield Corporate Bonds:** HY spreads at 350–420 bps over Treasuries reflect insufficient fear for an economy where payrolls just printed -92K and recession probability is elevated. In a hard landing scenario, these spreads widen to 600–800 bps, generating significant capital losses on top of duration risk. The return profile is asymmetric to the downside — you earn the carry if you're right and lose substantial principal if you're wrong. I would rather hold private credit (senior secured, 9–12% yield, direct lender protections) than the liquid high-yield market at current spreads.

- **Undifferentiated "Alternatives" Platform Products:** The major banks and asset managers (Blackstone BREIT, Apollo ARIS, KKR's retail vehicles) are aggressively marketing alternatives to retail and semi-institutional investors. These products carry real estate and credit exposure that is not being marked to market in real time, creating false stability in reported returns, but they also carry fee structures that consume a material portion of the illiquidity premium we are trying to earn. Being in a product where manager selection does not matter — where the entire strategy is fee extraction through retail distribution — is not the Yale Model. It is the corruption of the Yale Model.

- **Bitcoin and Speculative Digital Assets:** Bitcoin at $66,000 is a risk-on asset with maximum correlation to equity sentiment at exactly the moment we face recession risk, stagflation, and a live military conflict. I do not have a framework for valuing it. It produces no cash flows, no inflation-linked income, and no contractual return stream. The store-of-value thesis requires adoption to continue compounding — which is a narrative bet, not an investment. Gold has 5,000 years of demonstrated store-of-value characteristics and is doing its job right now. I will hold gold and real assets against inflation; I will not hold a speculative digital token and call it a hedge.
