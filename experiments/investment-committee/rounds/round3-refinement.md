## My Strongest Argument for Outperformance

The single strongest argument is the valuation dispersion between US large-cap equities (CAPE 36.7x, more than double the historical median) and international developed equities (12–14x forward earnings), combined with a dollar that is structurally weakening (DXY ~100, projected ~97.9 by Q4 2026). The historical parallel is 2002–2007: after the dot-com peak drove US CAPE to similarly elevated levels, the subsequent decade saw MSCI EAFE outperform the S&P 500 by cumulative 40–60 percentage points, driven by both valuation mean reversion and dollar weakness amplifying local currency returns for USD-based investors. Today the setup is structurally similar — US equities at historically stretched multiples, a weakening dollar, and international markets at historically cheap valuations — with the added catalyst of fiscal stimulus (European defense rearmament) and structural reform (Japanese corporate governance) providing fundamental earnings support. My portfolio's 18% international developed equity allocation, 12% energy equities, and 12% gold position collectively benefit from dollar weakness, oil at $100+, and the rotation away from US large-cap growth — three reinforcing tailwinds that do not require the Strait of Hormuz to close or the Fed to cut, simply for current conditions to persist.

## My Biggest Risk / What Would Prove Me Wrong

The scenario that proves me wrong is rapid US-Iran diplomatic resolution — say, a ceasefire announced in April or May 2026 — combined with a coordinated OPEC+ production increase that collapses WTI from $100 to $65–70/barrel within a quarter. In that scenario: my 12% energy equity position suffers 25–35% drawdown (energy stocks typically amplify oil price moves), my 12% gold position weakens as geopolitical risk premium unwinds and the dollar recovers (gold fell 20% from its January ATH in just two months), and the dollar strengthens on the relief rally, punishing my 18% international equity position in USD terms. The combined impact could be a 15–20% portfolio loss concentrated in a 60-day window — a scenario where Soros's put spread hedge would have been exactly right, and where my equity-biased, real-asset-heavy portfolio would have been exactly wrong. I hold this risk consciously: I am making a judgment that geopolitical risk is not fully priced and that dollar weakness is structural rather than conflict-driven. If I am wrong about both, this portfolio underperforms in the one scenario I am explicitly not hedging.
