We live in an era where information is infinite, AI can think faster than us, and everyone is optimizing. Everyone is playing it safe, hedging their bets, running the numbers. But here's the thing: the biggest wins in life don't come from spreadsheets. They come from doing the thing that no rational person would do.

What Is Retardmaxxing?

Retardmaxxing is the art of leaning into bold, unconventional moves — the ones that look stupid on paper but feel right in your gut. It's not about being reckless. It's about having the courage to ignore the noise, trust your instincts, and go all-in when the world tells you to diversify.

"The reasonable man adapts himself to the world; the unreasonable one persists in trying to adapt the world to himself. Therefore all progress depends on the unreasonable man." — George Bernard Shaw

Why Now?

AI is making everyone smarter, but it's also making everyone the same kind of smart. When every analyst has the same models, every founder reads the same playbooks, and every investor runs the same screens — the edge disappears. The new edge is being the person who zigs when everyone else zags.

The people who win aren't the ones who wait for perfect information. They're the ones who move fast, break things (including their own assumptions), and embrace the chaos.

The Rules of Retardmaxxing

  • Conviction over consensus. If everyone agrees with your bet, it's not a bet — it's a crowd.
  • Speed over perfection. A fast, imperfect move beats a slow, perfect one.
  • Asymmetric upside. Look for situations where you can lose a little but gain a lot.
  • Ignore sunk costs. If it's not working, kill it and move on. No ego.
  • Embrace looking stupid. The best ideas look dumb until they don't.

This isn't financial advice. This isn't life advice. This is a mindset. In a world where AI handles the thinking, sometimes the most powerful thing you can do is stop thinking and start doing.

So go ahead. Be unreasonable. Be bold. Be a little retarded. The world rewards it more than you think.
