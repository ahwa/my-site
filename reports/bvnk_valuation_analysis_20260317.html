Revenue Multiple Analysis

BVNK's implied revenue multiple at the $1.8B deal price depends on the revenue base used. With ~$40M 2024 revenue and estimated ~$80M 2025E revenue (assuming continued ~100% YoY growth):

EV / Revenue (2024) = $1,800M / $40M = 45.0x
EV / Revenue (2025E) = $1,800M / $80M = 22.5x
EV / Revenue (Base Deal) = $1,500M / $80M = 18.8x

High-growth fintech/crypto infrastructure companies have traded at 15–50x forward revenue in recent M&A. The 18.8–22.5x range on forward revenue is within — but toward the upper end — of comparable ranges.

Payment Volume Multiple

Valuing payments companies as a multiple of annualized transaction/payment volume (TPV) is standard for payment processors. BVNK processes $20B+ annualized.

EV / TPV = $1,800M / $20,000M = 0.09x (9 bps)
Base Deal: $1,500M / $20,000M = 0.075x (7.5 bps)

This implies Mastercard is paying ~7.5–9 basis points per dollar of BVNK's payment volume — a meaningful premium over traditional processors but justified by stablecoin's growth trajectory.

This 40 bps take rate is high relative to traditional payment rails (5–25 bps) but consistent with value-added stablecoin orchestration services that include compliance, multi-chain routing, and fiat conversion.

Why Mastercard Paid a Premium

1. Scale: $20B+ TPV is the largest among pure-play stablecoin infra — 4x Bridge at acquisition.

2. Enterprise Relationships: Worldpay, Deel, Flywire, dLocal — blue-chip fintech clients already using BVNK in production.

3. Revenue Maturity: ~$80M est. revenue with 163% net revenue retention — real business, not just volume.

4. Regulatory Positioning: Licensed in UK, EU, and South Africa; US expansion underway with 50-state coverage via Paxos partnership.

5. Competitive Defense: Bidding war with Coinbase drove price up; Mastercard needed to secure the asset before a competitor did.

6. Strategic Fit: Bridges Mastercard's fiat network with on-chain rails — uniquely complementary to the card network model.

Valuation Verdict

Across five valuation approaches, the $1.8B deal price (incl. earnout) falls within a reasonable range — neither a steal nor an overpay. The $300M contingent component provides downside protection for Mastercard.
