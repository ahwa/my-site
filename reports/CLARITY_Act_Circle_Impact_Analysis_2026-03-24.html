Executive Summary

The CLARITY Act's revised language on stablecoin yield threatens Circle's core growth narrative. While Circle's reserve income itself is not directly curtailed — the company earns interest on USDC-backing Treasuries regardless of regulation — the bill's prohibition on passing yield to holders removes a key demand driver for USDC. The primary transmission mechanism is through slower USDC circulation growth and a potential renegotiation of the Coinbase distribution arrangement, which currently absorbs ~63% of Circle's gross reserve income.

1. CLARITY Act: What Changed

On March 22-23, 2026, senators released the revised CLARITY Act text following a compromise brokered by Senators Tillis (R-NC) and Alsobrooks (D-MD), backed by the White House. The stablecoin yield provisions are the key new element that stalled the market structure bill for months.

Key Provisions

ProvisionDetailImpact
Passive Yield Ban Prohibits digital asset service providers (exchanges, brokers, affiliates) from offering yield on stablecoin balances — including structures "economically or functionally equivalent to bank interest" HIGH
Activity-Based Rewards Loyalty programs, promotions, and subscription-based rewards tied to stablecoin activity (payments, trading) remain permitted under defined conditions MEDIUM
Anti-Evasion Rules SEC, CFTC, and Treasury to jointly define permissible rewards and anti-evasion rules within 12 months MEDIUM
DeFi Lending Carveout Yield generated by third-party platforms (e.g., Morpho, Aave) from lending is NOT restricted — the ban targets issuer/affiliate-generated yield MITIGATING

Critical distinction: The bill does NOT prohibit Circle from earning interest on its reserves. It prohibits passing that yield to stablecoin holders. Circle's revenue is generated regardless — the risk is to USDC demand growth, not to the revenue mechanism itself.

Legislative Timeline

The GENIUS Act (signed July 18, 2025) already contains Section 4(c) prohibiting issuer-paid interest. The CLARITY Act extends this to affiliates and third parties acting on behalf of issuers. OCC rulemaking is expected by late 2026 to early 2027. The industry's legal challenge window is likely 6-12 months from final rule publication.

The Coinbase Revenue Share — Circle's Structural Burden

The Coinbase arrangement is critical to understanding Circle's economics. Under the current deal, Coinbase retains 100% of the reserve income generated from USDC held on its platform and splits income from other distribution channels 50/50 with Circle. In FY2025, distribution costs of $1.66B represented 63% of gross reserve income — meaning Circle keeps only ~37 cents of every dollar earned on reserves.

This arrangement was the price of securing Coinbase's distribution network, but it means Circle operates with structurally thin margins despite managing $75B+ in assets. FY2025 ended in a net loss of $70M even with $2.75B in top-line revenue.

3. Quantitative Impact Analysis

Transmission Channels

The CLARITY Act impacts Circle through three interconnected channels:

ChannelMechanismEstimated Impact
1. USDC Demand Growth Slowdown Without yield incentives, retail/institutional holders have less reason to hold USDC vs. bank deposits or money market funds. Coinbase's USDC rewards program (4.1-4.5% APY) was a major adoption driver. -15% to -30% growth rate reduction
2. Coinbase Distribution Renegotiation If Coinbase can no longer pay USDC rewards, it may demand a lower revenue share (no longer subsidizing user yield). Alternatively, Coinbase retains more margin by not paying out rewards — mixed impact. -5% to +5% on net revenue
3. Competitive Repositioning Tether (USDT), which has never offered direct yield, faces no incremental disadvantage. USDC's regulatory-compliance premium narrows. Simultaneously, Tether announced Big Four audit in March 2026, closing the trust gap. -5% to -10% market share risk

6. Strategic Implications & Mitigants

Mitigating Factors

FactorDetail
DeFi yield unaffected Lending protocols (Morpho, Aave, Compound) can still offer yield on USDC. Coinbase already launched on-chain USDC lending at up to 10.8% APY via Morpho — this is NOT restricted.
Activity-based rewards loophole The bill permits loyalty/promotion/subscription rewards tied to stablecoin usage. Coinbase One ($4.99/mo) already restructured USDC rewards under a subscription model in Dec 2025 — a potential workaround.
Regulatory moat deepens Compliance costs rise for all stablecoin issuers. Circle, as the most regulated issuer (with a US entity, state licenses, and Big Four audit), may actually benefit from higher barriers to entry.
Non-yield use cases growing USDC's cross-border payments, treasury management, and DeFi collateral use cases are yield-agnostic. USDC captured 64% of total stablecoin transaction volume in 2025.
Other revenue diversification FY2026E other revenue guidance of $150-170M (up from $110M in FY25) suggests Circle is diversifying beyond pure reserve income.

Risk Factors

RiskDetail
Tether competitive threat Tether announced a Big Four audit in March 2026, closing its main trust deficit vs. USDC. Since USDT never offered yield, it faces zero incremental regulatory risk from this bill.
Coinbase revenue share renegotiation If USDC rewards are curtailed, Coinbase's incentive to promote USDC diminishes. The 100% revenue share on Coinbase-held USDC may come under pressure — but in either direction.
Interest rate path With 96% revenue concentration in reserve income, any acceleration in Fed rate cuts compresses Circle's top line independently of regulatory risk.
Anti-evasion rules TBD The SEC/CFTC/Treasury have 12 months to define anti-evasion rules. The scope of what constitutes "economically equivalent to interest" remains unclear and could be interpreted broadly.

7. Conclusion

The CLARITY Act's stablecoin yield provisions represent a meaningful but manageable headwind for Circle. The ~18% stock drop on March 24 reflects the market's initial repricing of USDC growth expectations, but several mitigating factors — DeFi lending carveouts, activity-based rewards loopholes, and growing non-yield use cases — limit the downside.

Our base case fair value range of $89 to $123 suggests the stock is approximately fairly valued at ~$103 post-drop, with asymmetric risk to the upside if the bill is watered down or activity-based rewards are interpreted broadly. The bear case of $45-$86 represents a tail risk if enforcement is strict and USDC growth stalls, while the bull case of $125-$173 applies if the bill fails entirely.

The key variable to monitor is the SEC/CFTC/Treasury rulemaking over the next 12 months — specifically, how broadly "economically equivalent to interest" is interpreted and whether Coinbase's subscription-based rewards model survives scrutiny.
